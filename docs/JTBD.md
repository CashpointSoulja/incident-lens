# Jobs to be done

These jobs come from how revenue teams prepare for technical buyers. They have not been validated with incident.io's team. In a pilot, they would be the first thing to check with users (see [Rollout](ROLLOUT.md)).

## Core job

> Help me walk into a conversation with a technical prospect already knowing, from evidence I can defend, how they handle incidents today and where incident.io could help.

## Job stories

| # | When… | I want to… | So I can… | Served by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J1 | I am preparing for a first call with a technical prospect | see what is publicly known about how they handle incidents | open with something specific instead of "tell me about your stack" | Evidence ledger |
| J2 | I propose an incident.io product | have the reason and the source next to it | never claim something I cannot back up | Brief, product map |
| J3 | the prospect asks "what is this worth to us?" | have a business case whose assumptions we can change together | make the number belong to both of us | ROI levers |
| J4 | I hand an account to a colleague or a prospect | send one link that reopens exactly what I was looking at | avoid repeating the research or the numbers | Share link |
| J5 | I am qualifying an inbound or outbound account (BDR) | judge quickly whether incident response is a live topic for them | spend my time on the accounts that fit | Signals, hypotheses |
| J6 | I am planning expansion on an existing account (CSM) | see which parts of their public stack we do not yet touch | raise the next product with a reason | Product and integration map |
| J7 | I write or review sales-facing copy (marketing) | reuse product language that is sourced from incident.io's own pages | keep claims consistent and accurate | Product knowledge base, share mode |
| J8 | I own the revenue tooling (RevOps) | know which version produced a brief, and have its output in CRM-shaped fields | trust it, audit it and decide whether to wire it into the CRM | Brief versions, payload preview, kill switch |

## Functional, emotional and social dimensions

- **Functional:** cut research from many tabs down to one dated artefact, and separate fact from guess.
- **Emotional:** feel confident on the call; not being caught out by a claim you cannot source.
- **Social:** look prepared to a technical buyer, and hand over work that colleagues trust and reuse.

## Forces on switching

| Force | What it looks like here |
| --- | --- |
| Push (current pain) | Research is spread across homepage, status page, blog, careers and GitHub; notes are personal and unsourced |
| Pull (new solution) | One brief in seconds, every claim linked, a scenario and an editable business case ready to show |
| Anxiety | "Will it say something wrong about the prospect in front of them?" Addressed by the [honesty model](HONESTY-MODEL.md) and share mode |
| Habit | Existing call-prep docs and CRM notes. Addressed by a payload shaped for the CRM and a link that drops into existing notes |

## Hiring criteria

The tool is "hired" only if it is faster than manual research, is never wrong about a sourced fact, and has output that can go in front of a prospect without editing.
